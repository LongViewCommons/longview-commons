# Longview Commons

> Building practical independence through open technology, shared knowledge, resilient systems, and long-term stewardship.

## Why This Project Exists

Many of the systems people depend on are built around short-term profit, convenience, growth, and centralized control.

We rely on employers, corporations, cloud platforms, subscription services, communication networks, and technologies that we do not own and often cannot repair, understand, preserve, or replace.

These systems may be useful, but they are also fragile. A company can raise its prices, change its terms, remove access, discontinue a product, lose our information, or disappear entirely.

Longview Commons exists to help people build practical alternatives before those dependencies become crises.

## Our Mission

We help people build practical independence through:

- Open technology
- Shared knowledge
- Resilient systems
- Community cooperation
- Long-term stewardship

Our purpose is to replace fragile dependencies with alternatives that people can own, understand, repair, preserve, adapt, and share.

## What Practical Independence Means

Practical independence does not mean abandoning modern society or doing everything alone.

It means having choices.

It means having alternatives before a dependency becomes a crisis.

It means being able to preserve important knowledge, move your information, repair what you own, communicate through more than one system, reduce unnecessary recurring costs, and continue functioning when a corporation or institution changes direction.

## What We Intend to Build

Longview Commons will bring people together to create, test, document, fund, and share practical systems for ordinary individuals, families, and communities.

This work may include:

- Open-source and local-first technology
- Secure and resilient communication
- Personal data ownership and preservation
- Repairable and reusable equipment
- Local artificial intelligence
- Offline knowledge and educational resources
- Reduced subscription dependence
- Household resilience
- Community-supported projects
- Shared tools, skills, and infrastructure
- Long-term economic and technological planning

We will begin with practical technology and information systems while keeping the larger mission open to energy, water, repair, education, income resilience, and community infrastructure.

## Our Principles

### Essential Knowledge Should Remain Public

People may pay for support, training, implementation, convenience, physical products, and specialized services.

The foundational knowledge needed to understand and improve their own circumstances should remain publicly accessible.

### Privacy Is Structural

We will collect as little personal information as possible.

We will not sell or trade personal data.

People should be able to participate privately or anonymously where practical.

### Long-Term Stewardship Matters

A good system should remain useful, maintainable, affordable, secure, and understandable over years and decades, not merely convenient today.

### No Single Point of Failure

No single platform, corporation, account, payment provider, server, or individual should be able to erase or permanently disable the work.

Important public resources should be portable, backed up, and capable of being preserved by others.

### Political Independence

Longview Commons is nonpartisan.

We may discuss policies, laws, companies, and technologies when they affect privacy, repairability, public access, open standards, resilience, or long-term human control.

We will judge systems by their results, not by the political group associated with them.

### Financial Independence

Sponsors and donors may support the work, but they may not purchase conclusions, suppress criticism, control priorities in secret, or gain access to private community information.

### Public Work Should Remain Public

Openly funded guides, documentation, software, and public resources should remain available for others to use, preserve, improve, and share.

## Where We Are Starting

Longview Commons is beginning with an idea, one family, and the belief that this work is necessary.

We do not yet have a formal organization, staff, large audience, or all the answers.

We are starting by listening.

## Our First Question

> What system in your life do you depend on but do not truly control?

It could involve:

- Your work
- Files and photographs
- Communication
- Software subscriptions
- Devices
- Energy
- Transportation
- Finances
- Education
- Healthcare
- Household technology
- Important records

What would happen if that system suddenly became unavailable, unaffordable, or untrustworthy?

The answers will help us understand what people need, what useful solutions already exist, and what should be built next.

## Contributing

Longview Commons is not currently asking people to make major commitments.

Early contributions may include:

- Sharing an experience
- Identifying a fragile dependency
- Reviewing the mission
- Pointing to an existing solution
- Testing future guides
- Improving documentation
- Offering relevant knowledge
- Helping make resources understandable to ordinary families

Contribution guidelines and communication channels will be added as the project develops.
