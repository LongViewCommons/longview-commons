# Founding Statement

## Why I Am Starting This

Many of the systems people depend on are built around short-term profit, convenience, growth, and centralized control.

We rely on employers, corporations, cloud platforms, subscription services, communication networks, and technologies that we do not own and often cannot repair, understand, preserve, or replace.

These systems may be useful, but they are also fragile. A company can raise its prices, change its terms, remove access, discontinue a product, lose our information, or disappear entirely. Decisions that seem convenient today can create greater dependence, expense, waste, and vulnerability years later.

I believe we need to think beyond the next purchase, subscription, quarter, or election. We need to build systems that remain useful to our families and communities for decades.

## What I Believe

People should have greater control over the technology, information, infrastructure, and resources upon which their lives depend.

This does not mean abandoning modern society, rejecting technology, or expecting every household to become completely self-sufficient.

It means having choices.

It means having alternatives before a dependency becomes a crisis.

It means being able to preserve important knowledge, move our information, repair what we own, communicate through more than one system, reduce unnecessary recurring costs, and continue functioning when a corporation or institution changes direction.

Independence does not mean doing everything alone. Strong families and communities depend on cooperation, shared knowledge, complementary skills, and trustworthy relationships.

## What I Want to Build

I want to bring people together to create, test, document, fund, and share practical systems that ordinary individuals and families can use.

This work may include:

* Open-source and local-first technology
* Secure and resilient communication
* Personal data ownership and preservation
* Repairable and reusable equipment
* Local artificial intelligence
* Offline knowledge and educational resources
* Household resilience
* Reduced subscription dependence
* Community-supported projects
* Shared tools, skills, and infrastructure
* Long-term economic and technological planning

We will begin with practical technology and information systems, but the larger mission may eventually include energy, water, repair, education, income resilience, and community infrastructure.

## How the Work Should Be Done

Essential knowledge should remain publicly accessible.

People may pay for personal assistance, structured education, implementation, physical products, convenience, or specialized services. However, the foundational knowledge people need to understand and improve their own circumstances should not be locked away from those who cannot afford it.

The work should be:

* Practical
* Honest
* Secure
* Repairable
* Understandable
* Affordable over the long term
* Portable between systems
* Open to improvement
* Resistant to centralized control
* Documented well enough for others to continue

We should build for present needs while considering the people who may depend on the results decades from now.

## Independence and Integrity

This effort should remain nonpartisan and independent from corporate, political, governmental, and donor control.

We may discuss technologies, companies, laws, and policies when they affect privacy, repairability, public access, open standards, resilience, or long-term human control. We should judge them according to their results, not according to which political group supports them.

Sponsors and donors may support the work, but they must never purchase conclusions, suppress criticism, control priorities in secret, or gain access to private community information.

No single platform, company, server, payment processor, account, or individual should be able to erase the work. Important public resources should be portable, backed up, openly licensed where appropriate, and capable of being preserved by others.

## Where I Am Starting

I am beginning with an idea, my family’s support, and the belief that this work is necessary.

I do not have an organization, a staff, a large audience, or all the answers.

I am not presenting myself as an expert in every subject. My role is to identify important problems, bring together people with different knowledge and experiences, organize useful work, explain it clearly, and help ensure that the results remain available to the public.

I am looking for people who recognize this problem and believe that practical alternatives should be built.

The first question I want to ask is:

> What system in your life do you depend on but do not truly control?

The answers will help us understand what people need, what solutions already exist, and what we should build together.

## The Mission

We help people build practical independence through open technology, shared knowledge, resilient systems, community cooperation, and long-term stewardship.

Our purpose is to replace fragile dependencies with alternatives that people can own, understand, repair, preserve, adapt, and share.

Our long-term vision is a world where individuals, families, and communities possess the knowledge, technology, infrastructure, and relationships needed to remain secure and capable across generations.
