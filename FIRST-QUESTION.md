# What System in Your Life Do You Depend on but Do Not Truly Control?

Most of us depend on systems we do not own, cannot repair, and may not be able to replace.

A company can raise its prices, change its terms, remove access, discontinue a service, lose our information, or disappear entirely.

Longview Commons is beginning with a simple question:

> **What system in your life do you depend on but do not truly control?**

It could involve:

- Your job or income
- Files, photographs, or documents
- Communication
- Software subscriptions
- Devices or operating systems
- Internet access
- Banking or payments
- Energy
- Transportation
- Education
- Healthcare
- Household technology
- Important family records
- Something else entirely

## Why We Are Asking

We want to understand where ordinary people feel most dependent, vulnerable, trapped, or unable to make meaningful choices.

Your answer can help us identify:

- Common fragile dependencies
- Problems that are being ignored
- Existing alternatives that need better documentation
- Areas where current alternatives are too expensive or difficult
- Public resources that should be created
- Projects that people may want to build together

We are not asking because we already know the answers.

We are asking because useful work should begin by listening.

## Questions to Consider

You do not need to answer all of these.

- What system do you depend on?
- Who controls it?
- What would happen if it became unavailable?
- What would happen if its price increased significantly?
- Can you export or preserve what belongs to you?
- Can you repair or replace it?
- Is there an alternative?
- What prevents you from using that alternative?
- What would make you feel more secure or independent?
- What kind of guide, tool, service, or community support would help?

## How to Respond

For now, responses may be sent to:

**longviewcommons@gmail.com**

You may respond briefly or in detail.

Please do not include sensitive personal information, account credentials, private medical information, financial account details, or anything that could put you at risk if exposed.

Tell us whether:

- We may quote your response publicly
- We may quote it only anonymously
- You do not want it quoted

Unless you clearly give permission, we will treat your response as private and will not publish it.

## What Happens Next

Responses will help shape the first Longview Commons public resource:

> **The Practical Independence Assessment**

The assessment will help individuals and families identify important dependencies, understand the risks involved, and choose realistic first steps toward greater control and resilience.

## A Final Thought

Practical independence does not mean rejecting every outside service or doing everything alone.

It means having choices, knowledge, alternatives, and dependable relationships before a dependency becomes a crisis.
